Fireplace Guide

TV Mounting Above a Fireplace

A fireplace-mounted TV is one of the most requested installations in DFW living rooms. It can look great — but there are a few things to think through first.

Heat considerations

Heat rises, and electronics don't love sustained heat. With a gas fireplace producing normal, vented flame output, the wall a foot or more above the mantel is usually within safe temperature ranges. Wood-burning fireplaces can run hotter. A quick check: run the fireplace for 30-60 minutes and feel the wall at TV height — if it's too hot to comfortably keep your hand on, a TV shouldn't live there.

Viewing height

Fireplaces put TVs higher than ideal seated eye level. A tilting mount lets you angle the TV down toward the seating, which is why tilt mounts are the most popular choice for over-fireplace installations.

Wall construction

The wall above a fireplace is often framed with studs behind drywall — but can also be stone, brick veneer or tile. Each material needs the right anchors and drill bits. Confirm what your wall is before choosing hardware.

Cable routing

Fireplace walls usually have no outlet at TV height, so plan cable routing carefully. Options include routing wires behind the wall to a nearby outlet location, or an in-wall rated power solution installed by a professional. Cables should never be routed directly against hot fireplace surfaces.

Mount selection

Tilting mounts are the standard for fireplace installs. Full-motion mounts can also work where viewing angles vary. Because of the height and the distance of the TV's weight from the wall, the mount must be solidly anchored into studs.

Comfort

The best way to confirm comfort is to sit in your usual seat and have someone hold a piece of cardboard at the planned TV location. If your neck strains, lower the mount position or plan a stronger downward tilt. Comfort for long viewing sessions should win over aesthetics.
