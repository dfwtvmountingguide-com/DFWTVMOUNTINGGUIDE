85-Inch TV Mounting Guide

An 85-inch TV is a statement piece — immersive for movies and sports but heavy and wide enough that details matter.

1. Large TV considerations

85-inch screens are heavy — often 100–130 lbs or more. They need a heavy-duty mount, sometimes a two-step mount (mount to wall plate, then engage TV) to make installation manageable, and careful handling to avoid stress or damage.

2. Wall strength and structure

The wall and studs must support the TV's weight. For 85-inch installs, you'll almost always want wood studs, not just drywall or toggle anchors, and a mount that attaches to at least two studs. Masonry or concrete block is also viable for wall-mounting when the right anchors and drill bits are used.

3. Mount selection

For most 85-inch installations, a tilting heavy-duty mount is used; for large living rooms with separation or corner placement, a heavy-duty full-motion mount rated well above the TV weight is chosen. Cheap mounts are not an option on this scale — cost of failure is too high.

4. Handling & safety considerations

Because of the size and weight, professional installers typically mount these with two people and sometimes with a lift for the final lift to the bracket. Homeowners can dial it in, but careful handling, positioning and holding of the TV until the mount's safety latch is engaged are essential.

5. Viewing height

Because 85-inch TVs are so large, you can often mount them slightly higher without needing to tilt them out of view. A typical recommended height is center screen around seated height, plus some — 48–54 inches — though it depends on furniture and room. Keep in mind most of the screen should be within a comfortable, relaxing field of view without straining your neck.

6. Cable management & wire concealment

An 85-inch panel on the wall begs for a perfectly clean cable run. For straight walls, a powered in-wall cable kit is often best; for stone or brick, surface cord raceways are usually the cleaner choice.

7. When to choose professional installation

At this size, a professional install makes sense more often than not — the combined cost of the TV itself, a good mount, and the risk of dropping it mean most DFW homeowners get C&O TV Mounting or another trusted pro to do it. The result is cleaner and safer, with guarantees on the work.
