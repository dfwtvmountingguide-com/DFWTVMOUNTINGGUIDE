Wire Management

TV Wire Concealment Guide

Hiding the wires is what separates a quick TV mounting job from a polished one. Here's how the three options compare and what to choose for your DFW installation.

External cable management

The lowest-cost, simplest way to clean up wall wiring. A paintable PVC raceway or cord cover hugs the wall and conceals the cables without drilling through them. These are great for renters or when cutting into drywall is not an option. They look tidy — especially on smooth painted walls — and come in colors that match a typical DFW off-white wall.

In-wall wire concealment

The gold standard for a cleanly finished wall installation. Many new single-family homes and condos in the DFW area are wired this way. It involves drilling discrete holes, feeding HDMI and power behind the drywall, then using a recessed brush plate or outlet plates behind the TV and console for a finish-quality installation. Most buyers and Realtors will notice the difference immediately; it reads as true 'professional installation.'

When in-wall wire concealment may need additional considerations

A few conditions typically prevent a clean in-wall run: a fireplace wall that's already framed, a wall with a plumbing or electrical line at the planned cable path, a shared wall to a neighbor, or a very long run downstairs. A professional installer can flag these ahead and suggest alternatives — for example, a tilt-down mount to hide wiring or a powered in-wall TV wall outlet kit with proper fire-blocking.

Soundbar wiring

A wall-mounted TV often pairs with a soundbar. Keep the cables hidden under the wall. Consider using a low-profile HDMI cable run that comes out to the TV with a secure mount, and wrap loose HDMI cables inside a fabric tubing sleeve. A soundbar mount can also be installed so the soundbar stays directly under the TV, aligned and flush.

Streaming device wiring

Streaming devices tend to be small and easy to stash behind or beside the TV with a sticky pad or hidden media shelf behind the screen. Because remotes often use infrared, keep the device's emitter in line-of-sight to the viewer or use an IR extender cable to give you 100 feet or so of range. For a clean look, make sure the run isn't visible from the couch directly-facing angle.

Safety and code warning

Never run regular extension cords or un-rated power cords through drywall. Power cords must be code-compliant and contained in a fire-rated path with proper bushings and electrician-rated components. For these, consult a licensed electrician. Per the National Electrical Code, the wiring and power branched off to supply a device needs to be enclosed and protected. Working behind the wall may involve checking for studs, wires and existing insulation.

Pressing the finished look

The 'right' wire concealment depends on your home design, wall type, and budget. Most DFW customers choose in-wall when the wall is clear and the surface-mounted when drywall texture makes cutouts visible. In both cases, a professionally installed finish is usually worth the time and money.
