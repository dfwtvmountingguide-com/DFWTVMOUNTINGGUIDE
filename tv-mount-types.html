Mount Types

TV Mount Types: Fixed vs Tilt vs Full Motion

Choosing the right mount is the biggest factor in whether the final installation looks and feels right. Here's an unbiased comparison of the three most common mount types in the Dallas-Fort Worth area.

Fixed mounts

The slimmest, most budget-friendly option. A fixed mount attaches the TV close to the wall and does not move. Because it's simple, a fixed mount is usually faster to install than other types and lower-profile; this makes it work well on many framed, contemporary living room walls and bedrooms. Use a fixed mount if your TV stays centered on a viewing area and you never want to angle it.

Tilt mounts

Tilt mounts let you angle the TV down (typically up to about 15 degrees) and sometimes up slightly. The advantage is less glare when the TV is mounted higher than correct eye level, a common situation above a fireplace mantle. Because the mount allows the TV to tilt, it also saves space in the vertical stack above or below a low console.

Full-motion (articulating) mounts

These mounts pull the TV out further from the wall and swivel side-to-side. They're called 'full motion' because you can extend, tilt and swivel for very different views. These work well in corner wall installs, kitchens, bars, and anywhere people sit in multiple areas of the room. Because they come outward from the wall, the wall supports more of the mount, so the mount and anchors must be higher-quality and properly anchored.

Comparison: Fixed vs Tilt vs Full Motion

Fixed mounts: lowest profile, cleanest look, fastest to install, lowest cost. No adjustments later, so accurate pre-planning of height and location is key. Tilt mounts: slight angle adjustment, better for glare-free viewing above fireplaces or high walls. Slightly thicker/thicker console clearance. Full motion: maximum flexibility for pulling out, angling and turning for different viewers. Small trade-off: slightly higher weight/complexity, the TV needs a larger safe space, mount stability and quality matter more, and cost is higher.

How to choose the right mount for your DFW home

For most DFW bedrooms: a fixed or fixed/tilt mount, wall-mounted directly centered at the headboard. For most family rooms and living rooms: a tilting mount at the right seating height. If you often host or move for TV game or game day watching — a full-motion can let you pull a TV out of a corner to fit a larger seating arrangement. Small living room with many seating spots? Full motion can offer flexibility by angling the TV toward the largest viewing area.

Match the mount to the TV and wall

Every mount has a TV size and weight range. Before buying or using a mount, make sure the mount boy matches your TV's VESA size and weight and that it fits the structural conditions of your wall. An undersized mount or cheap fixed-type mount on a heavy 75-inch or 85-inch can look very bad if it sags or the anchors fail.
