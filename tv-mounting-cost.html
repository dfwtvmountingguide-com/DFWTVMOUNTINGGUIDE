Cost Guide

TV Mounting Cost in DFW: What You Should Expect to Pay

TV mounting costs vary because the job can vary a lot. Use this guide to understand the standard factors that go into a quote so you can make a confident, informed decision.

TV size and weight

The TV itself is a pricing factor. A 32-inch TV is very different from an 85-inch TV. Larger and heavier TVs need stronger mounts, better hardware, sometimes a second installer, and add time and liability. Because of that, the price generally scales with screen size.

Wall type

Mounting on drywall with wood studs is the standard. Drywall with metal studs is a bit more involved. Mounting on concrete block, brick, stone, tile or plaster often requires special anchors and equipment and can take longer — so the quote can be higher.

Mount type

A simple fixed mount is the fastest and lowest cost. A tilting mount adds a small amount, and a full-motion (articulating) mount costs more for hardware and installation time because the mount must attach to studs securely and support the TV's weight and range of motion.

Cable concealment

A visible cord is job-done, but many people prefer a clean, wire-free look. External cable management (surface raceways) is quick and cost-effective. In-wall wire concealment is a longer job and can cost a considerable amount more, depending on code, path drilling and whether a new outlet is required.

Number of TVs

If you are mounting multiple TVs (e.g. bedroom and living room, or four TVs for a commercial space), the price per unit generally comes down. Many companies offer a discount on the second and third TV because setup and travel are already being charged once.

Residential vs commercial

Commercial installations (restaurants, offices, gyms) often involve more coordination: multiple rooms, off-hours work, heavier duty mounts, stringing cable in public/co-working/shared spaces, and multiple units. These jobs tend to be quoted per project rather than per TV.

Permit and code considerations

For in-wall cable concealment and new power installations, some jurisdictions require permits or inspection. A professional company can advise or do this properly. It can affect both time and cost.

Why prices vary so much

Because the job is only partially about putting a mount on a wall. It's about the right mount for your wall and TV, the right height, secure anchoring, clean wiring and protecting your electronics. A $99 Facebook post and a $300 by-the-book install are not the same job — and they don't last the same either.

When does DIY make sense?

If you have a small TV, know your wall structure, can find studs reliably, and aren't trying to hide wiring or power behind drywall, you can DIY it. But for larger TVs (65-inch+), unusual walls, or wire concealment, paying a pro is often the practical move.
