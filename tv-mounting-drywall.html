Wall Guide

Can You Mount a TV on Drywall?

Yes — a TV can be mounted on drywall, but only when it's anchored into the wall structure behind the drywall, not just the drywall itself.

Understanding studs

Behind drywall are vertical wooden (or metal) studs, usually spaced 16 or 24 inches apart. Studs are the load-bearing part of the wall. The drywall itself is just a finished surface — it cannot safely hold a TV on its own.

Proper mounting hardware

The correct approach is to drive the lag bolts from the mount into the center of the studs. Use a stud finder to locate them, confirm edges, then drill pilot holes into the stud centers. Use the lag bolts and washers that come with a quality mount.

TV weight matters

A 32-inch TV might weigh 15 lbs; a 75-inch can weigh 100+ lbs. Heavier TVs put more stress on mount and anchors. Match the mount's weight rating to the TV, and use more anchor points (spanning two studs) for bigger TVs.

Mount rating

Every mount has a VESA pattern and weight rating. Confirm both before buying. The mount's rating should comfortably exceed your TV's actual weight — don't run at the mount's limit.

Why drywall anchors alone are not appropriate

Plastic or toggle drywall anchors transfer load only into the drywall panel, which is essentially pressed gypsum between paper. Over time, vibration, the weight of a tilted TV, or bumping the screen can pull the anchors out. This is how TVs end up on the floor. Anchors are fine for light decor, not for televisions.

When professional assessment helps

Older DFW homes, plaster-and-lath walls, metal studs, and walls with plumbing or wiring behind them all benefit from a professional look. A pro can identify what's behind the wall, pick the right hardware, and avoid costly mistakes.
