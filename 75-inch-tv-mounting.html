TV Size Guide

75-Inch TV Mounting Guide

The 75-inch TV is a popular home-cinema size in DFW. It looks great — but it's heavy, wide and needs proper planning.

1. Weight and mount considerations

A 75-inch TV typically weighs 75–120 lbs depending on model. You need a U.L.-rated mount with a weight rating comfortably above your TV's weight. Don't skimp on the mount — a sagging 75-inch is a bad look and a safety problem.

2. Wall structure & finding studs

A 75-inch is usually wide enough that the mount spans two wall studs. Use a durable stud finder to locate wood studs, then drill into the center of each stud, not the edge. In many Texas homes, a typical stud-framed drywall wall handles a 75-inch TV fine when anchored properly into studs.

3. Viewing height

A common comfortable starting point is to have the bottom of the screen around 40–42 inches from the floor when seated (roughly eye level for most sofas). For the bedroom you may want lower; above a fireplace you're often higher. Set the TV where you can see it comfortably for long viewing.

4. Mount selection

For most 75-inch installs, a tilting mount gives a nice low-profile look with a small downward tilt to reduce glare. A full-motion articulating mount is helpful if you need to pull the TV out, swivel between rooms, or move it closer for cleaning. Because of the weight, a well-built mount is more forgiving and lasts longer.

5. Drywall and mounting hardware

Drywall itself cannot support a 75-inch TV on its own; the mount must be anchored into hidden studs or solid masonry anchors. Toggle bolts and drywall anchors can sometimes help for smaller TVs, but are a risk for a 75-inch — use them only if the manufacturer rates them for the TV weight, and never substitute for a proper stud fix.

6. Cable management

A wall-mount is not finished until the wires are dealt with. On a 75-inch you almost certainly want a clean run — either a paintable cord cover (surface raceway) or full in-wall concealment with a power bridge and data passthrough.

7. Professional installation considerations

At 75 inches, most people find professional installation worth it, especially for things like: securing mount into studs properly, stress-testing before hanging the TV, routing HDMI cables cleanly, and pulling back behind the TV while spacing it correctly. A pro usually has two sets of hands, which is valuable on this size.
